This 1985 masterpiece from the king John Hughes is quite possibly a perfect movie - it’s also that rarest of films that can make you laugh and cry within its runtime, and it will leave you somewhat stunned in what you experienced. And supposedly, Hughes wrote it in like two days. Crazy. Just as with some of his other work, “Ferris Bueller” and “Planes Trains and Automobiles,” his storytelling prowess and comedic energy is unmatched - you think you’re watching a movie about high school or getting home for the holidays, but he taps into the deepest of human emotion to remind us who we are and how we can confront our fears, share them, and move through life while laughing and being honest all at once, and with some guidance and friendship from those around us, including and especially from who we may least expect to be able to help or relate. And the answer to that question, Mr Bender, next Saturday.
