This is one of the all-time greats. I realize Woody Allen has drifted into the distant past and is likely not well-known anymore. But living in NYC in the early 2000s, he was still somewhat of an icon, largely because he loved and lived in NYC (he played the clarinet weekly for jazz shows at the Carlyle hotel for many years into his seventies). I first saw this film in college and was immediately obsessed - with his humor, his inventive filmmaking, and the New York Jewishness on display. I simply had never seen anything like it. And while many have imitated, his style remains distinct and unique, especially amidst the evolution of movies in the last decades. See this one for the incredible wit, the young Diane Keaton bringing utter originality to the role, and the deep, wonderful wisdom that permeates the film, as we all seek our own “eggs” of life.
